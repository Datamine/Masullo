## G1. The real-place integrals used for Montel are unbounded

**§§1.4.2–1.4.3, pp. 28–29, and §§1.8.4.2–1.8.4.3, pp. 53–54. Concrete failure for the proposed nonnegative cutoff approximation.**

Take F = ℚ, the trivial character, and z = 3/2 in a compact set in the proposed domain containing this point. On the positive ray the limiting test function is t⁻³ᐟ² for t > 1. The positive-real-place summand in §1.8.4.3 has measure dt/t and kernel t¹ᐟ²/(t−1). Its limiting density with respect to dt, apart from the factor 1/2, is

```text
1 / (t² (t−1)).
```

On (1,2) this is at least 1/(4(t−1)), which has infinite integral. The regularized tests using a nonnegative mollifier and nonnegative bump vanish near t = 1 individually. Their pointwise limits on t > 1 and Fatou’s lemma still force the integrals to become unbounded. The trivial character gives the same sign on both real components. Lean proves the divergence for any measurable sequence with the indicated pointwise limit, using the positive part of its real density. For nonnegative tests this is the actual density. The result also applies to every subsequence satisfying the same convergence hypothesis. [Archimedean.lean](Archimedean.lean)

### Repair of the real-place limit

Fix a reference exponent z₀, for example a sufficiently large positive real value, and replace the one-sided limit on t > 1 by t⁻ᶻ − t⁻ᶻ⁰. With x = log t, the positive real component has density

```text
(exp((1/2 − z)x) − exp((1/2 − z₀)x)) / (exp(x) − 1).
```

The negative real component has denominator exp(x) + 1 when t = |g|. The Haar measure becomes dx, and unitary character factors have modulus at most one. The constant local normalization does not affect convergence. The numerator is O(x) at zero uniformly on bounded parameter sets, while exp(x)−1 ≥ x. On x ≥ 1 the denominator is at least exp(x)/2. The resulting exponential tail is integrable locally uniformly for Re z > −1/2 and Re z₀ > −1/2.

The replacement tests multiply the *difference* by a common smooth cutoff in log t vanishing near zero and infinity. The Lean development gives a common integrable envelope, uniform cutoff removal on compact parameter sets, and holomorphy of the limiting real-place integral in that half-plane. This resolves the real-place estimate for this modified family. The transfer of this subtraction to the rest of the proof remains under [G2](GAPS.MD#g2-the-spectral-limit-and-continuation-require-additional-estimates). [RealPlaceRepair.lean](RealPlaceRepair.lean)

## G3a. Replacing the Markushevich lifting step

## G23. Measuring horizontal growth in the contour height
