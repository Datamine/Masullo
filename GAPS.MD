## G2. The spectral limit and continuation require additional estimates

**§§1.8.4.3–1.8.5.1, pp. 54–55. Missing locally uniform control.**

For an individual zero ρ in the truncated strip, the proposed one-sided Mellin transform formally tends to 1/(z−(ρ−1/2)) in the indicated right half-plane. A termwise limit does not establish local boundedness of the entire spectral sum. Even if the finite-cutoff sums converge absolutely, the limiting terms in the imaginary direction decay only at reciprocal rate. A proof needs a summation convention or a regularized kernel, a zero-counting estimate with the relevant multiplicities, and locally uniform bounds for the cutoff family in the domain used for continuation. The words “a similar reasoning” on p. 54 do not supply these.

### Passing the subtracted family to the limit

Use the [reference-subtraction repair](OLD_GAPS.MD#g1-the-real-place-integrals-used-for-montel-are-unbounded), with the cutoff written in the log-norm coordinate as
$c_N(x)=\theta(Nx-1)\theta(N-x)$, where $N=n+1$ and $\theta$ transitions smoothly from zero to one on [0,1]. Fix a compact set of z to the right of the shifted strip and fix the reference exponent b in the same half-plane.

- **Spectral side.** Write $w=\rho-1/2=u+iv$. The cutoff transform is the integral of $e^{ivx}c_N(x)g(x)$, where $g(x)=e^{(u-z)x}-e^{(u-b)x}$. The bounded strip and compact parameter set give $|g^{(j)}(x)|\le C e^{-\delta x}$ for $j=0,1,2$, with $\delta>0$ independent of the zero. Also $g(0)=0$, so $|g(x)|\le Cx$ near zero. For $N\ge2$ the cutoff transition interiors are disjoint. On the lower transition $[1/N,2/N]$, its first and second derivatives are bounded by $C_1N$ and $C_2N^2$. The $g'c_N'$ and $gc_N''$ contributions have bounded integrals because the interval has length $1/N$ and $|g|\le 2C/N$ there. On the upper transition $[N-1,N]$ both cutoff derivatives are bounded independently of $N$. The term $c_Ng''$ is bounded by the exponential envelope. These estimates give a uniform bound on the L¹ norm of the second derivative of $c_Ng$. Two integrations by parts give $O((1+v^2)^{-1})$, with the L¹ bound for the undifferentiated profile used at small heights.
- A zero count $O(T\log(2+T))$, including multiplicities for the fixed character, makes the last bound summable by dyadic shells. Lean verifies the concrete derivative estimates above, the integration-by-parts bound, and uniform convergence of the concrete sums on bounded parameter sets with a positive distance in real part from the strip. It also verifies the dyadic summability comparison and holomorphy of the limiting resolvent sum given the count hypothesis. The corresponding zero count for the intended Hecke family and the identification of this regularized sum in the trace identity remain open. - **Complex place.** For the normalized squared denominator, let $r=|g|$ and $t=r^2$. The normalized angular average of $1/|1-r e^{i\theta}|^2$ is $1/(r^2-1)=1/(t-1)$, by the Poisson formula. This gives the G1 radial envelope also for the angular integral, since the modulus of the character factor is at most one. For an unsquared Euclidean denominator, $r-1$ is already a pointwise lower bound. With $x=\log r$ and normalized norm $t=r^2$, the latter estimate gives convergence on $\operatorname{Re}z>0$, which contains the intended half-plane. ## G5. Chapters 12 and 13 state different spectral and test-space formulas

**Theorems 12.5.1.1 and 13.3.1.1, pp. 519–522. Unjustified restatement.**

Chapter 12 uses zeros in the **interior** of a strip with their full multiplicities. Chapter 13 uses zeros **on the line**, replaces each weight by min(N(c), [F:ℚ]), and changes pointwise norm-one vanishing to Mellin-jet conditions. The manuscript gives no derivation of these changes. Plugging in the degenerate strip I = [0,0] under the printed interior convention yields an empty zero sum, as Lean checks. Obtaining the on-line identity instead needs a limiting or projection theorem, including boundary zeros, agreement of admissible tests, and justification of the clipped weights. [TraceAudit.lean](TraceAudit.lean)

If width zero is intended to be allowed literally in Chapter 12, the resulting identity fails over ℚ. In the trivial character component take a nonnegative smooth radial bump of height one at t = 2, with support shrinking to 2. The prime-2 local term from the norm-2 shell is fixed and positive. The other finite places have no prime-power norms in the support once it is sufficiently small. The two pole integrals tend to zero as the support shrinks. This contradicts the zero-width formula, whose spectral sum is empty and whose real local contribution is nonnegative. This calculation concerns the interior convention and the choice of width zero. It leaves the case of positive width to be justified.

Even for the trivial character, a nonnegative smooth test supported inside the positive norm ray away from 1 can vanish at 1 and have a strictly positive Mellin value at 0. Thus the Chapter 12 norm-one condition does not imply the first condition in Chapter 13. Lean checks this with a smooth test supported on [2,5]. [TraceAudit.lean](TraceAudit.lean)

The stated codimension also needs to be interpreted and established. The displayed constraints range over all unitary characters, and a bound on the number of jet coordinates for one character does not give that codimension for their joint kernel. Theorem 13.3.1.1 itself has a footnote requesting correction.
